NHS Dental Deserts Grow Amidst Inflation: 3.6% Impact on Access

Data refreshed 26 October 2026, 11:39 UTC

The latest inflation figures released by the Office for National Statistics indicate a CPIH annual inflation rate of 3.6% for September 2026, a statistic that reverberates profoundly through the landscape of NHS dental care. With the rising cost of living squeezing household budgets, the accessibility and affordability of dental services are becoming increasingly precarious. Many families are finding it harder to prioritise dental appointments, which could have lasting implications on public health as preventative care takes a backseat to immediate financial concerns.

The notion of NHS dental deserts—areas where access to dental services is severely limited—has been exacerbated by economic pressures. Data from the NHS shows that a significant portion of the population is living in regions where dental practices are under-resourced or altogether absent. These deserts are not merely geographical anomalies; they represent a systemic failure to meet the dental needs of communities, particularly in rural and economically deprived urban areas. The compounding effects of inflation mean that practices are also struggling to maintain staff and facilities, leading to longer waiting times and reduced service availability.

As households grapple with inflated prices across various sectors, including food and transportation, the decision to seek dental care often takes a backseat. The British Dental Association has highlighted the correlation between economic hardship and declining oral health, with many individuals foregoing necessary treatments due to their unaffordability. This creates a vicious cycle where poor dental health leads to more severe health issues, which in turn increases the burden on an already stretched NHS. The risk is that we may soon see a generation affected by preventable dental diseases, further exacerbating health inequalities in society.

Looking towards the future, the challenge of addressing these dental deserts amidst rising inflation demands urgent attention from policymakers. Innovative solutions, such as mobile dental units and community outreach programmes, could help bridge the gap in services. Additionally, there is an urgent need for government investment in NHS dental services, ensuring that financial constraints do not hinder access to care. As inflation continues to impact the economy, the health of the nation's teeth hangs in the balance, underscoring the importance of prioritising dental health as part of a broader public health strategy.
