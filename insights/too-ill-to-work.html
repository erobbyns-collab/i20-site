Long-term Sickness Drives UK Economic Inactivity to 20.9% in July

Data refreshed 23 October 2026, 18:56 UTC

The latest figures from the Office for National Statistics reveal that the UK economic inactivity rate for those aged 16 to 64 stands at 20.9% as of July 2026. This alarming statistic highlights a growing trend of long-term sickness keeping individuals out of the workforce, a phenomenon that is increasingly shaping the landscape of the British economy. With nearly one in five working-age adults sidelined due to health issues, the implications for businesses, public services, and the economy at large are profound and multifaceted.

A closer examination reveals that mental health disorders and chronic conditions are leading contributors to this alarming figure. Reports indicate that the pandemic has exacerbated pre-existing health issues, with many individuals experiencing burnout, anxiety, and other long-term ailments as they navigate the challenges of modern life. The stigma surrounding mental health continues to hinder open discussions, which in turn leaves many individuals without the support they need to reintegrate into the workforce. The social fabric of the nation is strained as families cope with the realities of lost income and disrupted lives, further complicating the issue.

The economic ramifications of this high inactivity rate cannot be understated. Businesses face challenges in finding skilled workers, leading to increased workloads for remaining staff and potential declines in productivity. Moreover, the tax base shrinks, constraining government resources available for healthcare and social services. This creates a vicious cycle: as economic pressures mount, the ability of the state to invest in mental health services and rehabilitation programmes diminishes, leaving those affected in a prolonged state of limbo. The government’s ongoing efforts to address this crisis will require not only targeted health interventions but also a broader examination of workplace culture and support systems.

As society grapples with these challenges, it is imperative to foster a more inclusive environment that prioritises mental and physical health in the workplace. This means not only enhancing access to healthcare services but also encouraging employers to adopt flexible working arrangements and mental health initiatives. Addressing the root causes of long-term sickness and creating a supportive work culture could help reverse the trend of economic inactivity. The stakes are high; unless we act decisively, the current rate of inactivity may become a permanent fixture in the UK’s labour landscape, stymying economic growth and diminishing the quality of life for millions.
