SEND Funding Crisis Deepens as Inflation Hits 3.6%

Data refreshed 26 October 2026, 11:39 UTC

The ongoing financial pressures on local authorities in the UK have reached new heights, with the latest Consumer Prices Index including owner occupiers' housing costs (CPIH) revealing an annual inflation rate of 3.6% for September 2026. This figure not only highlights the escalating cost of living but also underscores the urgent challenges facing Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) funding. As local councils struggle to maintain adequate support for vulnerable children, the implications of this inflation rate could be devastating for families relying on these essential services.

Local authorities are often the frontline responders to the needs of children with SEND, providing critical resources and support systems. However, with inflation rising, the purchasing power of council budgets diminishes, exacerbating an already precarious funding landscape. Many councils are reporting significant shortfalls in their SEND budgets, leading to cuts in services and support, which are often non-negotiable for families already facing a myriad of challenges. The 3.6% inflation rate only adds to the strain as councils attempt to stretch limited resources further, often at the expense of the very children they are mandated to support.

The implications of this funding shortfall are profound. Families are increasingly finding it difficult to access necessary provisions, from educational materials to specialist therapies, leaving many children without the support they need to thrive. The pressure on parents grows as they navigate complex systems to secure the services that should be readily available. The situation is a stark reminder of the broader societal implications of inadequate funding for SEND; it not only affects individual children but also places a burden on families and communities as they are forced to fill the gaps left by local authorities.

As we consider the future landscape of SEND funding, the growing inflation rate signals that immediate action is required to address these systemic issues. Policymakers must recognise the interconnectedness of inflation and public service funding, ensuring that adequate resources are allocated to meet the needs of children with SEND. This crisis presents an opportunity for a thorough reassessment of funding models and support structures, fostering an environment where every child, regardless of their needs, can access the education and support they are entitled to. Without such intervention, the reality of SEND funding may only worsen, leaving future generations at a significant disadvantage.
