The SEND Funding Squeeze

Data refreshed 14 September 2026, 11:03 UTC

As the UK grapples with the implications of a 3.1% CPIH annual inflation rate for July 2026, the education sector, particularly Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) funding, finds itself at a critical juncture. The rising cost of living has far-reaching effects on public services, and the educational landscape is no exception. Schools and local authorities are experiencing a funding squeeze that threatens to undermine the support systems essential for the most vulnerable children. Families relying on tailored educational provisions face increasing uncertainty as resources become scarcer, revealing the fragility of a system that is already under considerable strain.

The statistics paint a stark picture of the current landscape. With inflation outpacing wage growth for many, local authorities are finding it increasingly difficult to meet the rising costs associated with SEND provisions. This comes at a time when the demand for these services is surging, driven by increased awareness and diagnosis of special educational needs. As funding is stretched thinner, schools are having to make difficult decisions about resource allocation, often prioritising immediate operational needs over long-term investments in SEND support. This dilemma not only affects the quality of education that these children receive but also places immense pressure on teachers and support staff who are committed to providing the best possible learning environment.

The implications of this funding squeeze extend beyond the classroom. The well-being of families with children who have special educational needs is increasingly jeopardised as they navigate a system that is becoming less responsive to their needs. Parents are reporting heightened stress levels and anxiety, driven by the fear of inadequate support for their children. The psychological impact is significant; children with SEND who do not receive appropriate educational support may face long-term social and emotional issues, compounding their challenges and ultimately affecting their future prospects. This cycle of underfunding and unmet needs threatens to entrench inequalities, as families in less affluent areas may find it even harder to access the necessary support.

Looking towards the future, the SEND funding crisis will require urgent attention from policymakers. Addressing the systemic issues that have led to this funding squeeze is vital if we are to ensure that every child, regardless of their needs, has access to a quality education. The government must reconsider its funding formulas and provide adequate resources to local authorities, enabling them to meet the diverse needs of their populations. Moreover, a broader societal dialogue about the value of inclusive education is needed, encouraging a shift in perspective that recognises the long-term benefits of investing in SEND support. As the country faces economic challenges, the commitment to safeguarding the future of our most vulnerable children must remain a top priority, lest we risk perpetuating a cycle of disadvantage that could echo through generations.
