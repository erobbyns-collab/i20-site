The SEND Funding Squeeze

Data refreshed 04 September 2026, 13:42 UTC

The recent announcement of a 3.1% inflation rate as measured by the Consumer Prices Index including owner occupiers’ housing costs (CPIH) in July 2026 casts a significant shadow over the landscape of Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) funding in the UK. For a sector already grappling with budgetary constraints, these figures highlight the growing tension between rising living costs and the resources allocated to support vulnerable children. As inflation erodes the purchasing power of public funds, schools find themselves increasingly squeezed, struggling to provide adequate services for those who rely on additional support to thrive in educational settings.

This funding squeeze is not just a numerical abstraction; it translates into real-world consequences for students with SEND and their families. With schools facing tight budgets, there is often a prioritisation of essential expenditures over specialised services and support. Educational institutions may be forced to cut back on critical provisions, such as teaching assistants or tailored programmes, which can profoundly affect the learning experience of children with diverse needs. The disparity in available resources can lead to a deterioration in the quality of education for these students, exacerbating existing inequalities within the education system and leaving many families feeling abandoned by the very institutions designed to support them.

Moreover, the impact of this funding crisis extends beyond the classroom. With government budgets increasingly stretched, local authorities are finding it challenging to meet their statutory obligations under the Children and Families Act 2014, which mandates the provision of adequate support for children with SEND. When resources are insufficient, families face heightened stress and uncertainty, often resulting in disputes over Education, Health and Care Plans (EHCPs) or even the need for legal intervention. The emotional toll on families navigating these challenges can be immense, fostering a sense of marginalisation and helplessness that can linger long after educational barriers have been addressed.

As we look to the future, the implications of the current SEND funding squeeze are stark. Without a concerted effort to address the funding shortfall, the risk of further marginalising children with SEND looms large. Policymakers must grapple with the pressing need for sustainable funding solutions that are responsive to the realities of inflation and economic pressures. This is not merely an educational issue; it is a societal challenge that reflects our collective values and priorities. The ability to provide equitable education and support for all children, regardless of their needs, is a measure of a society’s commitment to inclusivity and social justice. As the dialogue around SEND funding continues, it is imperative that we advocate for a future where every child has the opportunity to succeed, free from the constraints imposed by financial limitations.
