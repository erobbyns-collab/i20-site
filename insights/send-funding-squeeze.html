SEND Funding Crisis Deepens as Inflation Hits 3.6% in September

Data refreshed 23 October 2026, 18:56 UTC

The recent announcement from the Office for National Statistics revealing a CPIH annual inflation rate of 3.6% for September 2026 has placed additional strain on the already beleaguered Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) funding. Local authorities, tasked with providing essential services for children with special needs, now find themselves grappling with rising costs while their budgets remain stagnant or are in decline. This inflation increase compounds existing financial pressures, leading to concerns over the adequacy and sustainability of SEND provision across the UK.

Underfunding of SEND services has been a persistent issue for years, but as inflation rises, the shortfall has become more pronounced. Many local authorities have reported a struggle to meet the needs of their SEND populations due to a lack of resources. With costs of everything from staff salaries to educational materials climbing steadily, the situation is dire. Councils are left with the difficult task of balancing their budgets while prioritising essential services, often resulting in cuts that directly affect vulnerable children. The ramifications of this funding crisis ripple through families and communities, creating a sense of uncertainty and anxiety about the future of education for children with special needs.

The pressures of inflation also intersect with broader socio-economic challenges. As the cost of living continues to rise, families are facing financial hardships that can disrupt their ability to advocate for their children's needs. Parents may be forced to make difficult choices, such as whether to pay for additional support services or manage their own expenses. This situation can lead to a disparity in access to necessary resources, where only those with means can afford supplementary help outside of what is provided by local authorities. The implications of this inequality are profound, as they reinforce existing societal divides and can hinder the potential of many children who require additional support.

Looking to the future, it is clear that the SEND funding landscape requires urgent reform if local authorities are to meet the growing demands placed upon them. With inflation persisting at levels that exceed the growth of public funding, advocates are calling for a comprehensive review of SEND financing structures. The government needs to acknowledge the interplay between inflation and educational funding, ensuring that local authorities are adequately resourced to provide the services necessary for all children. Without immediate action, the SEND crisis may deepen, further marginalising those who are already at risk of being overlooked in an educational system that should be inclusive and supportive for all.
